The problem we are exploring

Academic article records contain many details. Looking through them individually can make it difficult to identify articles that meet several criteria at once.

Our aim is to make this information easier to explore by combining keyword matching, filtering and sorting. For example, a user may want articles on a particular topic, published within a chosen period, with open access available.

Our dataset

We chose article metadata from OpenAlex, focusing on information systems and library and information science. The source sample covers publications from 2018 to 2020.

The dataset describes:

  • Article titles, DOIs, journals and publishers.
  • Publication years, research topics and keywords.
  • Authors, the first author's country and region, and international collaboration.
  • Open-access status, citation counts and citations per year.

The records are organised in XML so they can be read and processed consistently by the Python algorithm.

Why we chose this project

Academic article metadata gives us a practical way to connect the different parts of the assignment. We can examine how data is selected and structured, how an algorithm processes it, and how a website explains the project to its users.

It also encourages us to consider the limits of the data. For example, citations indicate recorded attention to an article, but do not establish its research quality on their own.

How we work

  1. Collect and clean the data

    Select article records, check missing information and organise the research topics.

  2. Structure the records

    Use consistent XML fields to describe each article and make the data ready for processing.

  3. Design the algorithm

    Develop Python logic for selecting and ordering articles according to the user's criteria.

  4. Build the website

    Prepare pages that introduce the project, explain the dataset and algorithm, and describe the team's work.
